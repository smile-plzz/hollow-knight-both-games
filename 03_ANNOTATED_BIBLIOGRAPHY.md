## 1. Direct Peer-Reviewed Studies on *Hollow Knight* and *Silksong*

### Grunberg, A. (2024). The ones who walk away from Hallownest: Hollow Knight's radical response to the Omelas dilemma. *Games and Culture*, 19(4), 433 to 448. https://doi.org/10.1177/15554120231166769
- **Disciplinary Focus:** Game Studies, Moral Philosophy, Utilitarian Ethics.
- **Methodology & Theoretical Framework:** Hermeneutic ludonarrative critique connecting Team Cherry's narrative architecture to Ursula K. Le Guin's parable *The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas* (1973).
- **Core Findings & Analytical Relevance:** Grunberg demonstrates that Hallownest's sovereign order (engineered by the Pale King) rests entirely upon a utilitarian contract of child sacrifice: sealing the infection within an agonizing, mutilated vessel (the Hollow Knight). While Le Guin's citizens face a binary moral impasse (either accept the social bargain or passively walk away into exile), *Hollow Knight*'s procedural interactivity provides the player with radical agency. The game's branching progression allows the player to reject mere substitution (Ending 1: The Hollow Knight), confront the systemic source of divine corruption (Ending 3: Dream No More), or harness the primordial Void to tear down the entire sacrificial architecture (Ending 4: Embrace the Void). Serves as a primary philosophical foundation for our ethical analysis of the Pale King's reign.

### Andriano, A. M., & Caracciolo, M. (2025). Metroidvania ecologies: Exploration and the environmental imagination in Hollow Knight and Rain World. *Eludamos: Journal for Computer Game Culture*, 16(2), 9 to 28. https://doi.org/10.7557/ejcgc.v16i2.7958
- **Disciplinary Focus:** Environmental Humanities, Ecocriticism, Metroidvania Spatial Design.
- **Methodology & Theoretical Framework:** Ecocritical analysis utilizing Timothy Morton's "dark ecology" and non-anthropocentric game studies to analyze player traversal in nonhuman post-apocalyptic ecosystems.
- **Core Findings & Analytical Relevance:** The authors examine how the spatial topologies of *Hollow Knight* resist conventional fantasies of anthropocentric mastery, resource extraction, and player dominion. Backtracking through the ruins of Hallownest is conceptualized not as repetitive ludic filler, but as an ecological loop wherein the player-avatar becomes embedded in an autonomous, indifferent, nonhuman biosphere. The decay of the Pale King's synthetic structures and the resurgence of untamed fungal and insect life illustrate the opacity and agency of more-than-human worlds. Crucial for our examination of Hallownest's spatial ecology and nonhuman world-building.

### Caracciolo, M. (2026). Playing a game of worlds: World building and its disruptions in video games. *Journal of Gaming & Virtual Worlds*, 18(1), 3 to 19. https://doi.org/10.1386/jgvw_00139_1
- **Disciplinary Focus:** Narrative Theory, Cognitive Media Studies, World-Building.
- **Methodology & Theoretical Framework:** Narrative analysis of world disruption and environmental precariousness across contemporary digital games, featuring comparative analysis of *Hollow Knight: Silksong* and *Elden Ring*.
- **Core Findings & Analytical Relevance:** Caracciolo investigates how video game worlds foreground systemic fragility rather than static fictional containment. In *Silksong*, the world of Pharloom is analyzed as a society under severe theological and material strain, where the vertical ascent toward the Citadel continuously exposes the fissures between the sacred myth of the Pilgrimage and the mechanical exploitation of insect labor and silk extraction. Provides a vital theoretical bridge linking Pharloom's vertical geography to narrative disruptions of institutional authority.

### Zhang, B. (2026). Research on the backtracking narrative mechanism in Metroidvania games. In *Proceedings of the 9th International Conference on Culture Technology (ICCT 2026)*. International Association for Convergence Science & Technology (IACST).
- **Disciplinary Focus:** Ludology, Game Design, Spatial Narratology.
- **Methodology & Theoretical Framework:** Structural and complex-network analysis of map layouts, traversal dependencies, and narrative gating in Metroidvania games, contrasting *Hollow Knight* with *Hollow Knight: Silksong*.
- **Core Findings & Analytical Relevance:** Zhang demonstrates that backtracking in Metroidvania games operates as a narrative delivery mechanism rather than a mechanical obstacle. In *Hollow Knight*, returning to previously explored chambers with new traversal abilities reveals new historical strata (e.g., discovering the infected state of the Crossroads). In *Silksong*, the delivery-based Wishes system and Bellway network tie backtracking directly to communal rebuilding, social intimacy, and the lived relationships of Pharloom's lower strata.

### Wysocki, J. (2026). Game review: Hollow Knight: Silksong (Team Cherry, 2025). *Journal for Religion, Film and Media*, 12(1). https://doi.org/10.25364/05.12:2026.1.11
- **Disciplinary Focus:** Religious Studies, Media Aesthetics, Theological Critique.
- **Methodology & Theoretical Framework:** Close hermeneutic reading of religious symbols, liturgical motifs, and ascetic practices embedded in *Hollow Knight: Silksong*.
- **Core Findings & Analytical Relevance:** Wysocki evaluates Pharloom's pervasive theological architecture, examining how the game deploys choral hymns, tolling bells, ritual spools, and liturgical vestments to construct an aesthetic of sacred obligation. The pilgrimage to the Citadel is analyzed as a spiritualization of institutional servitude, wherein devotion to Grand Mother Silk obfuscates somatic parasitism. The review highlights the tension between Hornet's secular, defiant agency and the pious surrender of Pharloom's pilgrims.

### Morales-Primo, A. U. (2026). Hornet's web: Weaving the biology of silk into Hollow Knight: Silksong. *Journal of Geek Studies*, 13(1), 29 to 36. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19487722
- **Disciplinary Focus:** Biological Sciences, Entomological Biomimetics, Game Studies.
- **Methodology & Theoretical Framework:** Comparative biomimetic analysis evaluating Hornet's ludic abilities against real-world arthropod silk biochemistry, dragline mechanics, and arachnid physiological morphology.
- **Core Findings & Analytical Relevance:** Morales-Primo provides a rigorous scientific assessment of silk as both material culture and gameplay mechanic in *Silksong*. The article details how silk's tensile strength, liquid-crystalline spinning, and elasticity are translated into Hornet's rapid traversal (Silk Soar, grappling needle) and restorative biology (Bind mechanic). It notes that Hornet's unique hybrid biology - born of the Wyrm (Pale King) and the Spider (Herrah the Beast) - embodies the evolutionary convergence of venomous predation and architectural weaving, grounding fictional game mechanics in authentic entomological principles.

## 2. Foundational Game Studies & Spatial Narratology

### Jenkins, H. (2004). Game design as narrative architecture. In N. Wardrip-Fruin & P. Harrigan (Eds.), *First person: New media as story, performance, and game* (pp. 118 to 130). MIT Press.
- **Annotation:** Jenkins conceptualizes game designers not as conventional storytellers, but as narrative architects who sculpt spaces pregnant with narrative potential. His four spatial narrative modalities - evocative spaces, enacting stories, embedded narratives, and emergent narratives - provide the core analytical framework for decoding how Hallownest and Pharloom encode historical collapse into their physical architecture.

### Aarseth, E. J. (1997). *Cybertext: Perspectives on ergodic literature*. Johns Hopkins University Press.
- **Annotation:** Aarseth introduces the concept of "ergodic literature," wherein the reader/player must expend non-trivial effort to traverse and reconstruct the text. In *Hollow Knight* and *Silksong*, narrative comprehension requires strenuous ludic labor: surviving brutal boss encounters, navigating lethal platforming gauntlets (e.g., the Path of Pain, Mount Fay), and actively synthesizing fragmented inscriptions.

### Bogost, I. (2007). *Persuasive games: The expressive power of videogames*. MIT Press.
- **Annotation:** Bogost's theory of "procedural rhetoric" - the practice of making arguments through processes, rules, and computational systems - is foundational for analyzing how resource management (Soul vs. Silk) and death penalties (recovering one's Shade or Shell Cocoon) procedurally argue for the fragility of consciousness and the material cost of survival.

### Juul, J. (2005). *Half-real: Video games between real rules and fictional worlds*. MIT Press.
- **Annotation:** Juul explores the dual nature of games as amalgamations of real formal rules and fictional, imagined worlds. Applied to Team Cherry's design, Juul's framework elucidates how abstract platforming rules (jump arcs, hitboxes, invulnerability frames) seamlessly coalesce with the fictional embodiment of insect agility and chitinous fragility.

### Fernández-Vara, C. (2011). Game spaces speak volumes: Indexical storytelling in video games. In *Proceedings of the 2011 DiGRA International Conference: Think Design Play*. DiGRA.
- **Annotation:** Fernández-Vara defines "indexical storytelling" as environmental signs left behind by past events (scratched walls, discarded husks, dried acid stains). This concept directly informs our spatial reading of Hallownest's corpses, forgotten shrines, and the archaeological traces of the Pale King's failed experiments in the Ancient Basin.

### Calleja, G. (2011). *In-game: From immersion to incorporation*. MIT Press.
- **Annotation:** Replaces ambiguous notions of "immersion" with a multi-dimensional model of player "incorporation" across spatial, tactical, performative, narrative, and affective axes, explaining how the player's visceral mastery of Hornet and the Knight produces profound affective attachment to ruined environments.

### Parish, J. (2018). *The anatomy of a genre: Video games and the Metroidvania evolution*. Boss Fight Books.
- **Annotation:** Documents the historical evolution of the Metroidvania genre, highlighting how gated non-linear progression, ability-driven expansion, and environmental mapping foster spatial intimacy between player and virtual territory.

### Wolf, M. J. P. (2012). *Building imaginary worlds: The theory and history of subcreation*. Routledge.
- **Annotation:** Establishes foundational parameters for secondary world subcreation, including invention, completeness, and consistency. Useful for evaluating how Team Cherry achieves extraordinary secondary-world coherence across two entire insect civilizations without relying on direct exposition.

## 3. Environmental Humanities, Ecocriticism & Arthropod Biology

### Chang, A. Y. (2019). *Playing nature: Ecology in video games*. University of Minnesota Press.
- **Annotation:** Chang articulates an environmental approach to digital game design, critiquing anthropocentric resource exploitation paradigms. Essential for analyzing how *Hollow Knight* and *Silksong* decenter the human subject, compelling players to navigate world systems governed by nonhuman metabolic, fungal, and insectoid rhythms.

### Morton, T. (2016). *Dark ecology: For a logic of future coexistence*. Columbia University Press.
- **Annotation:** Morton's "dark ecology" emphasizes the strange, shadowy, melancholic enmeshment of lifeforms within vast, interconnected biosphere systems ("the mesh"). Directly applies to Hallownest's infection - which is neither pure evil nor pure nature, but a luminous, parasitic psychic entanglement that collapses boundaries between life, death, dream, and decay.

### Bennett, J. (2010). *Vibrant matter: A political ecology of things*. Duke University Press.
- **Annotation:** Bennett's vital materialism theorizes the nonhuman agency and "thing-power" of inanimate and material substances. Provides a critical vocabulary for analyzing how the Void, Soul, and Silk operate not merely as passive magical currencies, but as active, vital material forces capable of autonomous resistance, collective uprising, and ontological transformation.

### Haraway, D. J. (2016). *Staying with the trouble: Making kin in the Chthulucene*. Duke University Press.
- **Annotation:** Haraway's theories of "sympoiesis" (making-with) and the "Chthulucene" (tentacular, subterranean, multi-species entanglements) offer profound insights into the Weavers of Deepnest and Pharloom. Silk is analyzed as a sympoietic technology that weaves together biology, architecture, kinship, and historical resistance against divine monoculture.

### Wilson, E. O., & Hölldobler, B. (1990). *The ants*. Belknap Press of Harvard University Press.
- **Annotation:** The definitive entomological text on eusociality, superorganism dynamics, pheromone communication, and colony defense. Provides the biological benchmark against which the social structures of the Hive (Queen Vespa), the Mantis Village, and Pharloom's ant/beetle labor brigades are evaluated.

### Vollrath, F., & Knight, D. P. (2001). Liquid crystalline spinning of spider silk. *Nature*, 410(6828), 541 to 548. https://doi.org/10.1038/35069000
- **Annotation:** Benchmark biochemical analysis of the phase-transition, tensile mechanics, and protein crystallization of arthropod silk. Grounding reference for evaluating the biomimetic plausibility of Hornet's needle-spun abilities and Pharloom's silk-based industrial technology.

## 4. Philosophy, Ethics, Divinity & Mythological Studies

### Le Guin, U. K. (1973). The ones who walk away from Omelas. In *The wind's twelve quarters* (pp. 275 to 284). Harper & Row.
- **Annotation:** The seminal philosophical parable exploring the moral bankruptcy of utilitarian social utopias built upon the deliberate, unceasing torment of a scapegoated child. Provides the ethical archetype against which the Pale King's creation and abandonment of millions of Vessel children in the Abyss is interrogated.

### Agamben, G. (1998). *Homo sacer: Sovereign power and bare life* (D. Heller-Roazen, Trans.). Stanford University Press.
- **Annotation:** Agamben theorizes sovereign power through the production of *homo sacer* - life that can be killed with impunity but not sacrificed within religious law ("bare life"). In *Hollow Knight*, the Vessels represent the ultimate incarnation of bare life: engineered outside civil citizenship, devoid of recognized kinship, and slaughtered wholesale to secure sovereign political permanence.

### Camus, A. (1942). *The myth of Sisyphus and other essays* (J. O'Brien, Trans.). Vintage Books.
- **Annotation:** Camus's philosophy of the absurd - the confrontation between human longing for meaning and the cold indifference of the cosmos - illuminates the player's experience of repetitive death, brutal failure, and unyielding persistence in the face of insurmountable divine and cosmic adversaries.

### Nagel, T. (1979). The absurd. In *Mortal questions* (pp. 11 to 23). Cambridge University Press.
- **Annotation:** Nagel analyzes absurdity as arising from the perpetual collision between the internal seriousness with which we view our commitments and the external perspective from which they appear arbitrary. Relevant to the tragic futility of Hallownest's Great Knights and Pharloom's devoted pilgrims.

### Eliade, M. (1959). *The sacred and the profane: The nature of religion* (W. R. Trask, Trans.). Harcourt, Brace & World.
- **Annotation:** Eliade's concepts of *hierophany* (the manifestation of the sacred), the *Axis Mundi* (the cosmic pillar connecting underworld, earth, and sky), and sacred geography directly elucidate the architectural ascent of Pharloom toward the Citadel and the vertical descent into Hallownest's Abyss.

### Otto, R. (1917). *The idea of the holy* (J. W. Harvey, Trans.). Oxford University Press.
- **Annotation:** Otto's characterization of the numinous as *mysterium tremendum et fascinans* (a mystery both terrifying and awe-inspiring) provides an apt phenomenological framework for the player's encounters with ancient higher beings (the Radiance, the Void Given Form, Grand Mother Silk).

### Ricoeur, P. (1984). *Time and narrative* (Vol. 1, K. McLaughlin & D. Pellauer, Trans.). University of Chicago Press.
- **Annotation:** Ricoeur's threefold mimesis (prefiguration, configuration, and refiguration) frames how players actively configure fragmented temporal artifacts into a coherent historical narrative of civilizational collapse.

## 5. Music, Atmosphere & Developer Archives

### Donnelly, K. J. (2014). *Occult aesthetics: Synchronization in sound film and video games*. Oxford University Press.
- **Annotation:** Examines the subliminal affective power of musical synchronization and dynamic acoustic cues in shaping uncanny, otherworldly, and dread-inducing interactive environments.

### Summers, T. (2016). *Understanding video game music*. Cambridge University Press.
- **Annotation:** Investigates video game music as an interactive, adaptive narrative system. Informs our analysis of Christopher Larkin's adaptive leitmotifs, dynamic instrumentation shifts, and structural silences across Hallownest and Pharloom.

### Larkin, C. (2020). *Christopher Larkin Doesn't Score Video Games, He Creates Worlds* [Interview by Casey Jarman]. *Bandcamp Daily*. https://daily.bandcamp.com/
- **Annotation:** Primary source interview with composer Christopher Larkin. Details his deliberate choice of live chamber instrumentation (solo viola, cello, piano, harp), dynamic layering, and melodic continuity between Hallownest's subterranean grief and Pharloom's choral, liturgical, and rhythmic string arrangements.

### Team Cherry. (2014). *Introducing Hollow Knight*. Team Cherry Developer Blog. https://www.teamcherry.com.au/blog/introducing-hollow-knight
- **Annotation:** Primary developer document outlining the founding ethos of Team Cherry (Ari Gibson and William Pellen), the evolution from the *Hungry Knight* game jam prototype, and the foundational commitment to traditional hand-drawn 2D animation and non-intrusive environmental storytelling.

### Team Cherry. (2017). *Hollow Knight then and now*. Team Cherry Developer Blog. https://www.teamcherry.com.au/blog/hollow-knight-then-and-now
- **Annotation:** Reflects on the launch of *Hollow Knight*, the expansion of Kickstarter stretch goals, the integration of Christopher Larkin's score, and the deliberate decision to preserve lore ambiguity and player-driven cartography.

### Team Cherry. (2019). *Hollow Knight: Silksong revealed*. Team Cherry Developer Blog. https://www.teamcherry.com.au/blog/hollow-knight-silksong-revealed
- **Annotation:** Official announcement documenting the transition of the second playable character (Hornet) from a Kickstarter DLC stretch goal into a fully realized, standalone sequel featuring an entirely new kingdom (Pharloom), new mechanics, and over 150 new enemies.

### Team Cherry. (2025). *Holiday 2025: Silksong launch and the road to Sea of Sorrow*. Team Cherry Developer Blog. https://www.teamcherry.com.au/blog/holiday2025
- **Annotation:** Key developer dispatch reflecting on the worldwide release of *Silksong*, post-launch patch updates, and officially detailing the thematic and artistic scope of the announced free expansion *Sea of Sorrow*.

### Australian Centre for the Moving Image [ACMI]. (2022). *From Ludum Dare to Pharloom: Ari Gibson and William Pellen on crafting Team Cherry's worlds*. ACMI Stories & Ideas. https://www.acmi.net.au/stories-and-ideas/from-ludum-dare-to-pharloom/
- **Annotation:** Authoritative curatorial interview documenting Team Cherry's design philosophy, environmental art direction, agile character animation, and the spatial contrast between Hallownest's descent into ruin and Pharloom's ascending theocracy.

### Nintendo Australia. (2018). *The metamorphosis of Hollow Knight with Team Cherry: Aussie developer interview*. Nintendo News & Articles.
- **Annotation:** Primary interview exploring the mechanical influences of classic platformers (*Zelda II*, *Faxanadu*, *Super Metroid*), the balance of high difficulty with atmospheric wonder, and the collaborative iteration of nail combat and charm builds.
