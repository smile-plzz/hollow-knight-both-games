# Lore Thread 04 (Silksong): The Weavers and Their Ancestral Fate in Pharloom

- **Topic:** The Weaver Guild, Cultural Exodus, and the Cradle of Threads
- **Game:** Hollow Knight: Silksong
- **Source Basis:** Secondary community transcriptions (`src_wiki_ss_characters`, `src_wiki_ss_areas`, `src_wiki_hk_weavers`), developer interviews, and narrative analysis.
- **Spoilers:** Revelations in Weavers' Cradle, Weaver Elder dialogue, and Hornet's lineage.

## 1. What the Games and Developers Say (Tier 1 Canon)
The origin of the enigmatic **Weavers** who inhabited Deepnest in *Hollow Knight* is definitively traced to the soaring kingdom of Pharloom [CLM-SS04-001]. Long before the construction of the High Citadel, the Weavers established their sacred ancestral sanctuary in the subterranean vaults known as the **Weavers' Cradle** [CLM-SS04-002]. 

In the Weavers' Cradle, the guild spun sacred silk from ancestral spindles, revering the **First Weaver** as their mythological progenitor [CLM-SS04-003]. When Grand Mother Silk and the Citadel clergy began centralizing all silk production into the grand Loom of Pharloom to enforce theocratic acoustic rule, a profound ideological and political schism erupted [CLM-SS04-004]. A significant faction of dissident Weavers refused to surrender their artisanal autonomy to the Citadel, undertaking the perilous Great Migration across the outer wastes to settle within the wild caves of Deepnest in Hallownest [CLM-SS04-005].

In *Silksong*, Hornet discovers that not all Weavers perished or fled: reclusive survivors, such as the hermit **Eva** (Old Weaver Eva) and the **Weaver Elder**, dwell in secluded sanctuaries within Pharloom's wilderness [CLM-SS04-006]. Upon recognizing Hornet as the daughter of Herrah the Beast and heir to Weaver heritage, Eva instructs Hornet in ancient threadcraft, unlocking powerful Weaver crests and advanced spool abilities [CLM-SS04-007]. However, Hornet must also confront the **Sister of the Loom** and spectral phantoms of fallen weavers who guard ancestral secrets against outsiders [CLM-SS04-008].

## 2. What the Evidence Strongly Implies (Tier 2 Interpretation)
The Weavers represent an artisanal indigenous guild whose sacred craft was co-opted and industrialized by an autocratic theocracy [CLM-SS04-009]. Their migration to Deepnest was an anti-imperial exodus: they chose the brutal, predatory darkness of the beast kingdom over the gilded slavery of Pharloom's towers [CLM-SS04-010]. Hornet's journey through Pharloom is fundamentally an archaeological return to her maternal roots: by exploring the Weavers' Cradle, she reclaims the living cultural knowledge that was suppressed when her mother entered the Dreamer slumber [CLM-SS04-011].

## 3. Interpretations and Gaps (Tier 3 and Disputed)
Scholars debate whether the Weavers in Deepnest eventually returned to Pharloom after Hallownest fell or if they were eradicated by the Radiance's plague [CLM-SS04-012]. The exact reasons why the Weavers left Hornet behind in Hallownest after her mother entered the slumber are also left open, suggesting either urgent flight or an intentional decision to let Hornet guard her father's ruins [CLM-SS04-013].

## 4. Why It Matters for the Project's Themes
The Weaver narrative anchors the intergenerational and anti-colonial themes of *Silksong*. It portrays craftsmanship not as an abstract aesthetic pursuit, but as a living site of political struggle over who controls the means of cultural production.
