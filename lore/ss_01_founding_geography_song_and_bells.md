# Lore Thread 01 (Silksong): Pharloom's Geography, Architecture, and Acoustic Culture

- **Topic:** Vertical Geography, Acoustic Domination, and Bell Hegemony
- **Game:** Hollow Knight: Silksong
- **Source Basis:** Secondary community transcriptions (`src_wiki_ss_main`, `src_wiki_ss_areas`, `src_wiki_ss_bosses`), developer interviews (`src_larkin_bandcamp_2025`, `src_tc_blog_silksong_rev`), and theological review (`src_wysocki_2026`).
- **Spoilers:** Details of Pharloom's vertical districts, Belltower, and Citadel layout.

## 1. What the Games and Developers Say (Tier 1 Canon)
Unlike the subterranean downward excavation of Hallownest, the kingdom of Pharloom is structured as an immense vertical spire ascending from damp cavern floors to sun-dappled mountain peaks [CLM-SS01-001]. The physical topography is divided into three distinct vertical zones:
1. **The Lower Depths:** Comprising the humid moss caverns of **Moss Grotto**, the chitinous shantytown of **Bonebottom**, the scorching bone furnaces of **The Marrow**, and the smoldering lava ports of **Deep Docks** [CLM-SS01-002].
2. **The Middle Threshold:** Comprising the sprawling, rainwater-swept moors of **Greymoor**, the towering bronze **Belltower**, and the acidic reservoir of **Bile Lake** [CLM-SS01-003].
3. **The Summit Metropolis:** Comprising the wealthy merchant avenues of **Gilded City**, the white marble sanctuaries of the **High Citadel**, the cathedral naves of the **Choral Chambers**, the clockwork archives of **Cogwork Vaults**, and the apex Loom at the **Peak of Song** [CLM-SS01-004].

Pharloom's societal governance is fundamentally acoustic: while Hallownest fell into silence, Pharloom is governed by song, choral liturgy, and colossal bronze bells whose acoustic resonance echoes through every shaft [CLM-SS01-005]. As composer Christopher Larkin explains, the acoustic score moves away from solo chamber melancholy to dynamic brass carillons, liturgical pipe organs, and choral polyphony that sonically enforce the theocratic majesty of the Citadel [CLM-SS01-006]. The ringing of massive bronze bells serves both as a civic timetable for enslaved laborers and an ideological instrument of mental disorientation that commands pilgrim obedience [CLM-SS01-007].

## 2. What the Evidence Strongly Implies (Tier 2 Interpretation)
The architecture of Pharloom physically embodies Jan Wysocki's (2026) theological analysis of somatic hierarchy: the vertical climb is designed as an agonizing ordeal of faith, wherein the lower classes endure heavy physical labor to extract raw minerals and bone to cast the very bells and spires that oppress them [CLM-SS01-008]. Spatial verticality functions as political subjugation: the High Citadel sits literally above the heads of the laborers, casting industrial pollutants and toxic runoff downward into Bile Lake and the Deep Docks [CLM-SS01-009]. Acoustic resonance replaces physical walls: the ringing bells create an intangible barrier that keeps pilgrims moving upward in uniform devotion while preventing revolt [CLM-SS01-010].

## 3. Interpretations and Gaps (Tier 3 and Disputed)
Scholars debate whether the song of Pharloom was originally a benevolent cultural hymn celebrating communal harvest before being weaponized by the Citadel or if it was conceived from its origin as a mechanism of mental control [CLM-SS01-011]. The structural age of the Belltower relative to the natural caverns is also unrecorded, suggesting an ancient foundation that pre-dates the current theocratic regime [CLM-SS01-012].

## 4. Why It Matters for the Project's Themes
Pharloom's geography represents the dialectical inversion of Hallownest. Where Hallownest forces the player to dig into the archaeological grave of an empire that died in silence, Pharloom demands an upward climb through an active, living totalitarian theocracy that rules through glorious sound and spectacle.
