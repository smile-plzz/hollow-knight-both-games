# Thematic Essay 05: Class, Labor, and the People Who Remain

## Introduction: The Ruins of Empire and the Working Class
In high fantasy narratives, kingdoms are usually examined from the viewpoint of kings, legendary knights, and cosmic deities. *Hollow Knight* and *Hollow Knight: Silksong*, however, maintain an intense, empathetic focus on the marginalized laborers, miners, shopkeepers, and outcasts who inhabit the lower rungs of society [CLM-HK03-006, CLM-SS08-004]. 

Drawing on Giorgio Agamben's (1998) analysis of bare life and Marxist spatial theory, this essay investigates how class inequality, forced labor, and economic extraction structure the ruined geographies of Hallownest and Pharloom. We demonstrate that while empires collapse and gods perish, it is the common people who endure, scavenge, and preserve communal life amidst the wreckage.

## 1. Hallownest's Subterranean Underclass
Beneath the glittering black stone spires of the City of Tears ran a brutal, hidden division of labor [CLM-HK07-001, CLM-HK07-002]. While wealthy nobles took private elevators to luxury dining rooms and frequented Marissa's singing performances in the Pleasure House, the essential maintenance of the city was forced upon disposable underclasses [CLM-HK07-002]:
- Maggots, regarded as the lowest of common bugs, were subjected to humiliating manual labor, forced to clean sewage drains and beaten by armored sentries [CLM-HK03-006]. When one maggot stole Hegemol's ornate armor to become the False Knight, his motive was not imperial conquest, but a desperate desire to protect his vulnerable brethren from being slaughtered [CLM-HK03-003].
- In the Royal Waterways, the sewage canals were relegated to flukes and waste scavengers, directly below the royal promenade [CLM-HK07-007, CLM-HK07-010].
- In Crystal Peak, working-class miners like Myla chipped away at singing crystals to power the capital's street lamps, inhaling crystal dust until the infection consumed their minds [CLM-HK06-006].

When the capital fell, the Pale King and his court vanished into the dream realm, leaving the working-class husks to wander the rain-soaked streets in eternal, repeating labor loops [CLM-HK03-008, CLM-HK06-011].

## 2. The Bone Mines of Pharloom: Theocracy as Industrial Plantation
In *Silksong*, class exploitation is not an abandoned relic of the past; it is an active, humming industrial machine [CLM-SS08-001]. The Marrow functions as an industrial bone quarry where pilgrims who lack funds to pay the exorbitant gate tolls are condemned to forced labor in the blast furnaces [CLM-SS08-002, CLM-SS08-004].

Supervised by the brutal Iron Warden, laborers like Drudge crush mineralized chitin and smelt bronze bells day and night, their lungs ruined by ash and their hearing shattered by the relentless tolling of shift bells [CLM-SS01-007, CLM-SS08-003, CLM-SS08-007]. The theocracy justifies this cruelty through theological deceit, assuring workers that their physical agony is a sacred sacrifice that clears their spiritual debt [CLM-SS08-009]. The High Citadel's golden domes and marble courtyards are literally cast from the crushed bones of these disposable laborers [CLM-SS08-008].

## 3. The Flea Caravan and the Grassroots Economy of Mutual Aid
Against this bleak industrial cruelty, Team Cherry posits the grassroots resilience of common bugs [CLM-SS11-001]. In *Silksong*, the town of Bonebottom and the Flea Caravan represent an alternative social order based on mutual aid, craft, and solidarity [CLM-SS09-008, CLM-SS11-003]:
- Independent artisans like the Forge-Daughter in Deep Docks and the Pinstress in Bonebottom work not for the Citadel, but for the common traveler, forging weapons out of salvaged scraps [CLM-SS09-008, CLM-SS12-011].
- The Flea Caravan, led by the charismatic Flea Master, creates a vibrant mobile sanctuary [CLM-SS11-003, CLM-SS11-004]. Rescuing lost fleas restores joy and community to Bonebottom: the encampment expands, music plays, and the Flea Cook serves nourishing broths [CLM-SS11-005, CLM-SS11-010].
- The Wishes system allows Hornet to act as a community defender, clearing supply corridors, delivering medicine, and protecting vulnerable wanderers [CLM-SS11-002, CLM-SS11-009].

## 4. What We Do Not Know (Epistemic Boundaries)
We do not know whether the maggot population survived outside the Crossroads, or if the False Knight's brethren were completely wiped out following his defeat [CLM-HK03-006]. In Pharloom, the economic fate of the Marrow miners following the destruction of the Last Judge and the collapse of the Loom is left open, posing the critical question of whether post-theocratic Pharloom can establish a fair communal distribution of resources.
